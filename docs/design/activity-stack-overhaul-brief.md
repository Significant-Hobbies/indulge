# Activity stack overhaul

## Job and desired outcome

Help a person recognize the combination that repeatedly takes more time than
they want. The step should feel like assembling a truthful little portrait of a
habit, not completing a category grid.

The success moment is immediate and visual: television creates the sofa and TV;
scrolling puts a phone in the character's hand; a drink occupies the next
believable surface; removing one choice removes only its corresponding object.

## Scene grammar

1. One foundation owns environment, furniture, pose, camera, and primary prop.
2. Compatible additions attach to left hand, right hand, lap, side table, or an
   ambient room socket.
3. Preferred sockets resolve first; fallbacks are stable and explicit.
4. Two props never occupy one socket.
5. Incompatible foundations remain separate scene variants until the next step
   asks which one tends to keep the person longest.
6. The control layer and physical scene always describe the same membership.

Example: `TV -> living room + sofa + television`; `Scrolling -> phone in right
hand`; `Smoking -> left hand`; `A drink -> side table when both hands are busy`.

## Direction probes

### E2. Powder Sky + Cherry (selected; delegated)

The selected implementation direction keeps the distilled scene, one question,
edge-peeking horizontal gallery, and Continue structure. It replaces the
rejected green, gold, cream, and plum interface with powder blue, clean white,
cherry red, and navy ink. Generated 3D work becomes bundled product imagery,
not a mockup that the runtime approximates with symbols.

Reference: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-e2-powder-cherry.png`

- E1: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-e1-daylight-cobalt.png`
- E3: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-e3-ink-tangerine.png`

### D. Distilled object shelf (current recommendation)

The scene, one question, one horizontal object shelf, and Continue are the only
layers. A full-size trailing choice remains partially visible so the scroll
gesture is self-evident. Selection summaries, counts, instructional copy, and
the duplicate vertical list are removed; the assembled room is the feedback.

Reference: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-d-distilled-shelf.png`

### A. Living Scene Composer (recommended)

The scene occupies the upper half; a quiet cream sheet below shows the exact
scene membership and a scannable additive list. This makes 20–30 indulgences
discoverable while keeping the causal animation obvious. It is the strongest
balance of magic, clarity, accessibility, and implementation control.

Reference: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-a-scene-composer.png`

### B. Story Deck

The first foundation is chosen, then one large conversational card at a time
asks what tends to join it. This is warmer and more guided, but slower for an
exhaustive catalog and less efficient when a person already knows their stack.

Reference: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-b-story-deck.png`

### C. Room Palette

The room becomes the interface and objects appear to lift from a bottom palette
into their physical sockets. This is the most magical direction, but it carries
the highest discovery, legibility, Dynamic Type, and asset-production risk.

Reference: `artifacts/design/directions/activity-stack-c-room-palette.png`

## Boundaries

- Keep the time-loss framing; do not ask what the person generally enjoys.
- No wordmark masthead, yellow progress bar, opening Skip, green-gold chrome,
  selected-count-only feedback, floating activity badges, or generic card grid.
- Preserve one question at a time, native accessibility, Reduce Motion, and
  local-only profile state.
- Generated probes are composition north stars, not literal final screenshots
  or permission to ship generated art unchanged.

## Approval state

The owner explicitly approved putting generated high-quality imagery into the
application and delegated the exact image choice. E2 is selected for build.
